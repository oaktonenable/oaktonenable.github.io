Who We Are

ABOUT
THE CLUB

Oakton Enable is where biology meets engineering, where empathy drives design, and where high school students prove that age is no barrier to solving medicine's hardest problems.

We operate in the space between clinical need and technical possibility - choosing problems rooted in real human experience, and building solutions with dignity at the center of every design decision.

"We exist to engineer hope - for the patients who wait, for the families who struggle, and for a future where transformative technology is accessible to everyone who needs it."

Design Ethos

SUSTAINABLE & ACCESSIBLE

We design our products to be biodegradable, environmentally responsible, and economically accessible to the people who need them most. Unlike many commercial devices that remain out of reach because of their high cost, we focus on practical, low-impact solutions that can serve more communities without sacrificing care, quality, or dignity.

IsoWrite: a smart writing companion for tremor stability

IsoWrite is our flagship project - a student-designed assistive writing tool built to reduce the impact of Parkinson's-related tremor and help users write with steadier, more natural control.

Evidence Before Engineering

RESEARCH
LIBRARY

Every mechanism we design starts with a question we can't answer from intuition alone. This is where we track what we're reading, what we're testing, and what we still don't know.

Focus Areas

Tremor Biomechanics

Understanding the frequency, amplitude, and variability of Parkinsonian hand tremor - the baseline every stabilization mechanism has to be measured against.

Signal Filtering Methods

Comparing approaches to separating intentional motion from involuntary oscillation in real time, from simple smoothing to more adaptive filtering logic.

Assistive Device Ergonomics

How grip, weight distribution, and feedback affect whether an assistive tool actually gets used day to day - not just whether it works on a bench.

Patient & Caregiver Input

Conversations and community feedback that shape our priorities - making sure we're solving problems people actually have, not ones we assume they have.

How We Work

FROM LITERATURE TO PROTOTYPE

Stage 01
Literature Review
We start with published research and clinical resources to understand what's already known.
Stage 02
Synthesis
Findings get distilled into design constraints - numbers and patterns our engineering has to respect.
Stage 03
Applied Testing
We validate assumptions against our own prototypes, not just against the paper they came from.
Stage 04
Documentation
What we learn - including what didn't work - gets written up for the next iteration.
